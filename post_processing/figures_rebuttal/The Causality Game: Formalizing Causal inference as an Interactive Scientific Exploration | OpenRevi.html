The Causality Game: Formalizing Causal inference as an Interactive Scientific Exploration

Sergio Andrés Amortegui Ardila, Felix Mohr

05 May 2026 (modified: 28 May 2026)NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations and Datasets Track SubmissionEvaluations and Datasets Track, Senior Area Chairs, Area Chairs, Reviewers, AuthorsRevisionsCC BY 4.0

Abstract: Causal inference seeks to uncover cause-effect relationships across domains including the natural sciences, economics, and education. In many settings, reliable causal conclusions require interventions, but interventions are costly and must therefore be selected strategically. Existing benchmarks largely evaluate methods on fixed datasets, sometimes including interventional observations, but don't allow agents to choose experiments, adapt to observations, and stop when further evidence is not worth its cost. We introduce The Causality Game, an interactive benchmark that addresses these shortcomings and evaluates causal agents by both inference quality and interaction cost. We also provide thecausalitygame, an extensible Python implementation of this protocol. The package includes built-in missions, metrics, implementations of over 31 standard strategies, and over 50 benchmark tasks for immediate use across diverse causal settings.
